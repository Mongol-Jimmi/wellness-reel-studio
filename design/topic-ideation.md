# Wellness Topic Ideation

## Divergence

Five independent frames generated 30 candidates: evidence regulator, gentle game design, biology, inversion, and curious everyday questions.

## Scoring

Candidates were scored from 0 to 10 with novelty weighted 35%, viability 40%, and Plain-Spoken Pebble fit 25%. Near-duplicates, weak evidence, medical or product risk, inaccessible actions, anxiety bait, and poor 30 to 45 second explainability were flagged before selection.

10. The One-Study Headline Check, 8.13

The tenth selection replaces the higher-scoring product-label candidate because it has lower medical and product risk and strengthens the channel's evidence culture.

## Deepened top three

### Plan the Restart, Not the Streak

A pre-decided minimum restart may reduce in-the-moment decisions after a routine lapse. Research must distinguish general implementation-intention evidence from direct lapse-recovery evidence. Safe wording uses “may make restarting feel easier,” never treatment or guaranteed consistency.

### The Mental Tab Handoff

A short external return note may reduce how much unfinished context must be held during an interruption. Research must separate laboratory resumption-cue studies from everyday outcomes and avoid ADHD-specific claims without ADHD-specific evidence.

### Shrink the Choice Menu

Showing fewer immediate options may lower decision load for some situations. Choice-overload findings are mixed, so the Reel must present this as an optional experiment and preserve useful flexibility.

## Non-obvious viable pick

The One-Study Headline Check is less like a conventional wellness tip, but it makes evidence literacy part of the brand rather than invisible backstage work.

## Wildcard

Could every evidence-backed Reel briefly show the plain-language question that the team used to test its central claim?
