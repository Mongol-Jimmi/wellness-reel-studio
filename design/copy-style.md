# Plain-Spoken Pebble Copy Style

## Voice

Warm, direct, adult, and easy to say out loud. The copy should sound like a thoughtful person, not a wellness app or a clinical handout.

## Rules

- Do not use em dashes.
- Prefer contractions and ordinary words.
- Keep one idea per sentence.
- Explain unfamiliar terms before giving advice.
- Use optional language: “If you’d like,” “You could try,” or “A good place to start.”
- Never promise a cure, reset, guaranteed result, or instant relief.
- Do not imply that the viewer failed if an exercise does nothing.
- Use a specific source label for statistics.
- Read every line aloud and remove anything that sounds polished but unnatural.
- Keep captions short enough to understand at normal playback speed.

## Humanizing pass

Before rendering:

1. Read the complete script aloud.
2. Replace jargon with the phrase a friend would use.
3. Remove em dashes, stacked slogans, and repetitive sentence patterns.
4. Check that transitions explain why the next step matters.
5. Ask whether every instruction feels optional and realistic.
6. Preserve the exact meaning of sourced claims and statistics.

## Example

Avoid: “Optimize your sleep environment to improve restorative outcomes.”

Use: “A cool, quiet, dark room is a good place to start.”
