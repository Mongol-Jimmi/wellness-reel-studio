# Step Back: one-minute play-along practice

**Duration:** 60 seconds
**Voice:** OpenRouter `openai/gpt-audio-mini`, Coral
**Format:** Plain-Spoken Pebble practice, loop aware
**Exercise:** self-distancing, done by picturing the event and putting it into words

## What the viewer does

The practice happens in the silences. The viewer brings one small irritation to mind,
watches it from a few steps back as though it were happening to someone else, then
quietly describes what that person is dealing with and what they would do next.

## Why it is built this way

The 2022 meta-analysis pooled 48 studies and 102 effect sizes on self-distancing and
found a small overall effect on emotional response (Hedges' g = -0.26, 95% CI -0.36 to
-0.15). One moderator mattered: the visual and verbal approach, where people both
picture the event from outside and articulate it, outperformed the visual-only approach
and the pronoun-swap approach on their own.

That finding is the design. A Reel that only said "call yourself by your name" would be
the weakest version in the evidence. So the minute asks for both halves: look at it from
outside, then say what you see.

## Screen

Two pebbles. Yours in periwinkle, the thing in coral, pressed against each other at the
start. Through the minute they ease apart and the coral one shrinks slightly, the way
anything does with distance. While the viewer describes what they see, small mustard
marks cross the gap. On the close the two drift back together, so a replay begins from
the same picture it started with.

Words on screen for the whole minute: `step back`, `keep it small`, `look`, `say it`,
`what next`, `again?`, and the source chip.

## Evidence and safety

- Source: Reflect on emotional events from an observer's perspective: a meta-analysis of
  experimental studies, *Cognition & Emotion* (2022),
  https://doi.org/10.1080/02699931.2022.2134094
- The effect is small. The Reel says the two halves together worked better than either
  alone, which is what the moderator analysis showed, and claims nothing more.
- `keep it small` is the safety boundary, on screen during the opening. This is a general
  wellness practice for everyday irritations, not a method for processing trauma.
- A viewer who finds the memory distressing can stop and let it go. Nothing in the minute
  asks them to stay with something painful.
