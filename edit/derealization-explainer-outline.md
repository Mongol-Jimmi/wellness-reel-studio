# Plain-Spoken Pebble: Derealization Explainer Outline

**Status:** next-script outline, not rendered
**Target:** 35 to 45 seconds
**Goal:** explain the experience before offering an optional grounding check

## Proposed structure

### 0:00 to 0:05 | What it can feel like

**Copy:** “Derealization can make the world around you feel foggy, distant, or unreal.”

**Visual:** The ROOM pebble drifts slightly out of focus while the speaker’s words remain sharp.

### 0:05 to 0:11 | What the term means

**Copy:** “That feeling has a name. It does not mean you are making it up.”

**Visual:** DEREALIZATION appears in plain type, then breaks into the simpler words DISTANT, FOGGY, UNREAL.

### 0:11 to 0:16 | Why the exercise follows

**Copy:** “A grounding exercise cannot diagnose or cure it. It may help bring your attention back to what is happening around you.”

**Visual:** The blurred ROOM pebble becomes steady while FEET, SOUND, and NOW enter.

### 0:16 to 0:32 | Optional grounding check

1. “If you’d like, notice where your feet or body meet a stable surface.”
2. “Name one sound near you.”
3. “Let one breath move only as deeply as feels comfortable.”
4. “Notice what feels present right now. No required result.”

### 0:32 to 0:40 | Help-seeking context

**Copy:** “If this keeps happening, feels frightening, or gets in the way of daily life, talk with a qualified healthcare professional.”

**CTA:** “Save if useful.”

## Language boundary

- Do not say “fix derealization,” “return to reality instantly,” or “reset your nervous system.”
- Do not imply one exercise works for everyone.
- Use optional language throughout.
- Keep the definition separate from diagnosis.
- No em dashes.
