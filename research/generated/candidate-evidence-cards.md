## FEET • SOUND • NOW

**Topic:** `sensory-grounding`

### Easy steps
1. Feel where your feet or body meet a stable surface.
2. Name one thing you can see, one you can hear, and one you can feel.
3. Pause and notice whether your attention has shifted; stop if this feels worse.

**Safety boundary:** This is an optional grounding prompt, not medical treatment for derealization or dissociation.

## AIR • EASY • SLOW

**Topic:** `gentle-breathing`

### Easy steps
1. Sit, stand, or lie somewhere supported.
2. Let the breath move into your belly only as deeply as is comfortable—never force it.
3. Breathe gently in through the nose and out through the mouth; count evenly if helpful.

**Safety boundary:** A short Reel can introduce the practice, but it is not an instant reset or a complete breathing session.

## NOTICE • RETURN • NOW

**Topic:** `mindful-noticing`

### Easy steps
1. Choose one present sensation, such as a sound or the feeling of your hands.
2. Notice it without deciding whether it is good or bad.
3. When attention wanders, gently return once—no score and no failure.

**Safety boundary:** Mindful noticing is a general wellness practice, not a substitute for care.

## HANDS • RELEASE • REST

**Topic:** `muscle-release`

### Easy steps
1. Pick one comfortable muscle group, such as your hands.
2. Gently tense without straining, then release.
3. Notice the contrast; skip injured or painful areas and stop if uncomfortable.

**Safety boundary:** Avoid painful areas or forceful tension; this is a brief introduction, not individualized treatment.

## LOOK • COLOR • TEXTURE

**Topic:** `nature-microbreak`

### Easy steps
1. Step outside or look toward a natural view if one is available.
2. Notice one color, one texture, and one sound.
3. Treat any change as information, not a required outcome.

**Safety boundary:** This is a low-pressure wellness experiment; evidence and context still require full-text review.

## WIDE • DETAIL • WONDER

**Topic:** `awe-noticing`

### Easy steps
1. Notice something that feels vast, intricate, or surprising.
2. Describe one concrete detail rather than forcing a feeling.
3. Let the moment be neutral if awe does not appear.

**Safety boundary:** Treat awe as an emerging research lead, not a guaranteed mood intervention.
